About Johannes

In for the long haul.

At first glance, deep tech and long-distance triathlon have nothing in common. Look closer and they share almost everything. Both take years to pay off, and both punish shortcuts. The hardest part of either isn't the start or the finish. It's the long, quiet middle, where nobody is watching.

I learned the physics first, then spent a decade building quantum computers and frontier AI. Training for Ironman and Xtreme triathlon taught me what that work never spelled out: pacing beats intensity, recovery is part of the plan, and discomfort is information, not a stop sign.

A question of mindset

Putting in the boring grunt work.

Long-distance endurance sport isn't something you do on the side. It reshapes your weeks: early sessions, sleep, food, a year planned backward from race day. Building a deep-tech company asks for the same commitment. The launch and the finish line get the photos. Most of the work happens in between, in the unglamorous middle, and that's where you find out who you are.

Philosophy

Discomfort ≠ Danger

Untrained, your body sounds the alarm at about 65% of max heart rate. Trained, it stays calm up to about 80%. Every block of training moves that line, at work too: more load, same clear head.
